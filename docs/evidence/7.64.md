# PLAN 7.64: the ferry for a nation fronted through an ally alone (ADR-617)

2026-10-21, on HEAD `e759a88f` (before) and the rule (after). Tools: `tools/diag/islandArmies.ts`
(new read `noOwnFront`: the island formations of nations with no front cell of their own; 60 days

`7.64/before_y*.json`, `after_y*.json`), `tools/diag/warSides.ts` (30 days of each:

(`7.64/profile_99.log`).

## 1. The rule, in two halves

`invasionAi` (`ai/invasion.ts`):

- **(a) The ally's front.** An actor at war with no front cell of its own takes as the ferry's front
  its allies' cells beside the enemies it fights with them (`wars.together(n, ally, enemy)`). Before,
  `planFerry` was called only with the nation's own front cells, so such a nation was never ferried.
- **(b) A landing that ships nobody leaves the turn.** `board` returns whether it gave an order, and
  `planLanding` returns that. Before, a landing with transports at a port and an enemy port to sail
  for took the turn even when nobody stood at the port or on its land (`board` did nothing). For a
  nation with no front cell of its own every war is "a war with no front", so the landing always
  went first: on the before's 27 watched such nations (5 or more island formations), 970 of their
  1,521 nation-days ended in that dead end (`gates.noneFree`), 211 had no transports at a port of
  their own, 245 sent formations to the port.

Neither half moves anything alone: without (a) the ferry is not called; without (b) it never gets
the turn. The unit test is red without each.

Test: `ferry1938.test.ts`, a new case. The United Kingdom and its allies at war with Italy; every
British cell beside an enemy handed to an ally (Britain holds no front cell); its army on the
fronts' lands gone; its transports at a British port in Africa; a division at Glasgow. With the
rule the transports sail for a British port on Britain within STAGGER days; with either half
taken out, they do not.

The bar (under half of HEAD's, under 425) is **missed**.

What the ferry does now, the watched nations (5 or more such formations on day 1; before → after,
island formations day 1 → day 60, boarded `b`, landed `l`): the Netherlands' nine cases move by 1–3
each (1129 y10 6 → 4, b2 l1; 7247 y10 12 → 9, b3 l3; 1307 y10 8 → 6, b2 l2), Australia 3613 y10
8 → 5, Britain 2347 y20 8 → 4, the United States 5683 y20 212 → 211 (b2 l1, two groups, 44,800
men of room). Nothing moved for Germany 8693 y10 (15) and Japan 5683 y20 (35, 2,000 men of room).

Why it misses: **the transports**. A nation so placed has, as a rule, one transport group of 24,000
men (one or two divisions) and a voyage of weeks between its island and its allies' front; in 60
days a group makes one trip or two. Two of the watched (the Netherlands and the British Raj on 8693
y20) have no transports at all. The want that buys transports for the ferry (`wantsFerry`,
`ai/economic.ts`, ADR-614) reads the nation's own front cells as the ferry did, so a nation fronted
through an ally alone never orders a group for it: PLAN 7.66. The rest of the fall (850 − 696 = 154,
of which the watched nations' ~35) is the unwatched tail of nations with 1–4 such formations.
