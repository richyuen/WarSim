## 1. The rule

`musterPoint` (`systems/production.ts`, ADR-47): a nation at war with no front cell of its own takes
as its front its allies' cells beside the enemies it fights with them (`wars.together`, the ferry's
front, ADR-617). A new land formation is raised at its city (owned and controlled) on a landmass of
those cells nearest one of them; with none there, at its port with water nearest one of them by sea
(`seaKm`), by its city in the port's cell, else the port's cell; with no port, at home as before.

Test: `ferry1938.test.ts`, a new case. The United Kingdom and its allies at war with Italy, every
British cell beside an enemy handed to an ally, the British capital moved to an islet of its with
no port (its spawn point there, asserted). The muster is at a British city on a land of the allies'
front; with those cities' cells handed to the ally, at a British port with water off those lands
and off the islet. Red without the rule (the islet), and without the port's branch alone.

## 3. Why it misses

The figure counts standing island formations; the rule changes where new ones appear, and few
appear in 180 days of a year-10 or year-20 world. The 793 of day 1 are carried off only by the
ferry, a transport group's room a voyage (7.64, 7.66). Where the rule bites, a nation's new
divisions stand on its allies' front's land or at a port where the ferry boards, not on a home
islet. What keeps such armies at home now is the capital's place (PLAN 7.68) and the AI's reach
of an islet capital (7.69).
